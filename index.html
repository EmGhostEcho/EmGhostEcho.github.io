GhostEcho: Electromagnetic Tampering Threats on
Portable Ultrasound Imaging and AI-Assisted Medical Diagnosis

Paper #XXX, submitted to NDSS 2027

Abstract

Diagnostic ultrasound is widely employed in clinical practice owing to its safety, portability, and real-time imaging capability. Despite its critical role in patient care, the security of ultrasound imaging against electromagnetic (EM) attacks has received insufficient attention. In this paper, we present GhostEcho, the first closed-loop, real-time electromagnetic tampering system targeting medical ultrasound imaging. GhostEcho captures unintentional EM emanations from the device's display interface to recover live screen content as visual feedback, and injects attacker-controlled artifacts into the ultrasound image via intentional electromagnetic interference (IEMI). The system further incorporates automatic parameter discovery to identify the EM leakage frequency and estimate the target's internal pulse timing without prior knowledge, enabling deployment against unfamiliar devices. Using the reconstructed display as feedback, GhostEcho adaptively adjusts artifact positions to track scene changes such as probe movement. Evaluation demonstrates that the forged artifacts are visually consistent with genuine pathological structures and can mislead both AI-based diagnostic models and clinicians, potentially fabricating or concealing pathologies and compromising clinical decisions. These findings expose a previously unrecognized security risk in medical ultrasound systems.
